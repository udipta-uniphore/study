# ESL Chapter 2: Overview of Supervised Learning

## Key Concepts

- **Statistical decision theory:** Choose a predictor $f$ to minimize expected prediction error over the joint distribution of $(X, Y)$.
  - Related: [[stat101]] §13 (full derivation)
- **Expected prediction error (EPE):** $\text{EPE}(f) = E[(Y - f(X))^2]$ for squared-error loss. It's a population quantity, so it can't be computed directly.
- **Regression function:** The best predictor under squared error is the conditional mean $f^*(x) = E[Y \mid X=x]$.
  - Intuition: the average of $Y$ among all cases with $X = x$.
- **Irreducible vs. reducible error:** $\text{EPE}(f) = E[\text{Var}(Y\mid X)] + E[(f^*(X) - f(X))^2]$. The first term can't be removed; the second is the gap between $f$ and $f^*$.
  - Related: same Pythagorean split as bias-variance ([[stat101]] §10).
- **Linear least squares as a special case:** Restricting $f(x) = x^T\beta$ gives $\beta = \Sigma_{XX}^{-1}\Sigma_{XY}$ with centered variables.
- **Other losses:**
  - Absolute error: the minimizer is the conditional median.
  - 0-1 loss for classification: the minimizer is the Bayes classifier, which picks $\arg\max_g \Pr(G=g\mid X=x)$.

## Quick Definitions

- EPE = expected prediction error
- $f^*(x) = E[Y\mid X=x]$ = regression function
- $\Sigma_{XX}$ = covariance of the inputs
- $\Sigma_{XY}$ = covariance between inputs and output

## Derivation: Why the Conditional Mean Minimizes EPE

**Step 1: condition on $X$.** Since $\Pr(X,Y) = \Pr(Y\mid X)\Pr(X)$,
$$\text{EPE}(f) = E_X\Big[E_{Y\mid X}\big[(Y - f(X))^2 \mid X\big]\Big]$$
The outer expectation averages over $x$, so it's enough to minimize the inner term for each $x$, treating $f(x)$ as a constant $c$.

**Why $E_X$ is outside:** the joint average can be done in two stages (law of total expectation).
- Inner: for each fixed $x$, average over $Y$ given $X=x$.
- Outer: average those results over the distribution of $X$.
- Analogy: average each class's test scores first, then average the class averages.
- It helps here because, after the inner step, each $x$ has its own term in $c = f(x)$. You can pick the best $f(x)$ separately for every $x$. Swapping the order would bury $f(x)$ inside the $X$-average, which blocks that.

**Step 2: minimize pointwise.** Let $\mu(x) = E[Y\mid X=x]$. Then
$$E\big[(Y-c)^2 \mid X=x\big] = E\big[(Y-\mu(x))^2 \mid X=x\big] + (\mu(x)-c)^2$$
The cross term vanishes because $E[Y - \mu(x)\mid X=x] = 0$. The first term doesn't depend on $c$, and the second is minimized at $c = \mu(x)$.

**Result:** $f^*(x) = E[Y\mid X=x]$, with minimum error $E[\text{Var}(Y\mid X)]$.

## Derivation: Linear Case

Restrict to $f(x) = x^T\beta$ and minimize $E[(Y - X^T\beta)^2]$. The gradient is
$$-2\,E\big[X(Y - X^T\beta)\big]$$
Setting it to zero gives the normal equations $E[XX^T]\beta = E[XY]$, which say the residual is perpendicular to $X$. So
$$\beta = \big(E[XX^T]\big)^{-1}E[XY]$$
With centered $X$ and $Y$, this is $\beta = \Sigma_{XX}^{-1}\Sigma_{XY}$. It requires $\Sigma_{XX}$ to be invertible, which needs positive definiteness ([[stat101]] §4).

## Key Equations & Intuition

$$\text{EPE}(f) = E\big[(Y-f(X))^2\big] = E[\text{Var}(Y\mid X)] + E\big[(f^*(X)-f(X))^2\big]$$
- Intuition: total error = unavoidable noise + distance from your predictor to the best predictor.
- When used: comparing predictors on the same population; explaining why no method can beat the noise floor.

$$f^*(x) = E[Y\mid X=x]$$
- Intuition: the best prediction is the local average of $Y$.
- When used: target for nearest-neighbor and regression methods, which estimate it from data.

$$\beta = \Sigma_{XX}^{-1}\Sigma_{XY}$$
- Intuition: regression coefficients are covariance ratios; this is the matrix form of $a = \text{Cov}/\text{Var}$.
- When used: linear regression in ESL §3.

## Main Takeaways

- The best predictor under squared error is the conditional mean, not the mean of $Y$.
- EPE splits into noise that can't be removed and error that a better predictor could remove.
- Linear regression is the best linear predictor, computed from covariances.
- EPE is a population target; training error is only an estimate of it, which is why bias-variance matters.
